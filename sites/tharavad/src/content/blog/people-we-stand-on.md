None of this is original. We are standing on a long line of people who
thought a computer should be a tool you could fully own.

- **IBM and the mainframe shops.** They built multi-user machines when
  "sharing a computer" was the only way most humans would ever touch one.
  Time-sharing, terminals, remote login: the idea that a box could be a
  place, not only a desk ornament.

- **Bell Labs and Unix.** Small tools, composable interfaces, the assumption
  that users are adults.

- **The free software and open source people.** Source you can read. Systems
  you can rebuild. A culture that still believes understanding is part of
  using.

- **Public access Unix, tilde clubs, shell accounts, university clusters.**
  Generations of nerds who gave strangers a home directory and a prompt
  because that was how you learned.

- **Forum elders and mailing-list ghosts.** The ones who answered the same
  question for the tenth time without turning it into content.

Tharavad is a small thank-you to that lineage, and a small door for whoever
is 13 (or 31) and still wishes they had a box. We cannot reverse the feed.
We can still keep a house open. TinkerHub put the roof on; the rest of us
get to bang on the walls.
